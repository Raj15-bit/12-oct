Why the rupee matters most

  • Oil — India's crude import bill rose 48% in April to August while it bought slightly less oil.
  • Shipping — freight costs are up more than 400% since the war began, so even cheaper crude arrives dear.
  • Yields — a safe US bond pays about 5.24%, close to the 5.8% "earnings yield" (profit divided by price) on Indian shares.

Reading between the lines, the RBI is spending reserves to buy time until oil or US yields ease. One view is that this is the strongest defence since 2013 and that ₹97 is the line it will hold. The opposite view, from Emkay Global economist Madhavi Arora, is that these are tactical fixes, not a cure, because the oil bill does not shrink.

The price of money and the rupee

The RBI is raising rates and rationing dollars while US borrowing costs sit at 20-year highs.

In short
  • The RBI raised its main rate to 5.50% and turned to a tightening stance.
  • It then made betting against the rupee costly and opened a dollar window for oil companies.
  • US long-term borrowing costs are the highest in over 2 decades, which keeps pressure on India.

What the RBI did on Saturday

After raising the repo rate (the rate at which it lends to banks) by 0.25%, the RBI went after speculation in the currency market. The rupee had closed at 96.73 per dollar, just short of its record low.

Views split on what comes next. One view: this is the strongest support in over a decade, and the RBI will not let the rupee pass ₹97. The opposite view: the oil companies need $11-12 billion a month, so the RBI is just moving that demand onto its own reserves.

  • Importers — bankers say the 20% cash rule makes honest hedging very costly, not just speculation.

  • Bonds — India's 10-year government bond yield hit 7.37%, the highest in 3 years.

Reading between the lines, the RBI has chosen a stable rupee over cheap money. That helps importers of oil but hurts every company that borrows, and long-term bond funds were the worst performers of the week.

Views split on the auction too. One view: demand was the strongest in years, with 80% going to investors outside the dealer banks, so yields may stop rising. The opposite view: the high price buyers demanded proves lenders want more for lending long.

  • Treasury plan — Citi expects the US Treasury to sell fewer long bonds and more short bills from next month.
  • Fed odds — markets give about a 17-20% chance of another Fed hike at the 27-28 October meeting.

  • Hartnett — Bank of America's strategist argues that a Fed hike would now be the most bullish move for markets.

So the world is paying more for money exactly when India needs foreign money most. Until US yields fall, cheap Indian shares can stay cheap.

03

Oil, ships and war

The fighting reached Saudi airports and Hormuz tankers, and the cost of moving oil hit records.

In short
  • A Houthi missile strike on Riyadh's airport killed 12 people and injured 309.
  • Iran's navy said any ship near Hormuz with its tracker switched off is hostile, and tankers burned.
  • Hiring a supertanker now costs a record, so Asia pays the most for its oil.

The Gulf under fire

Yemen's Houthis hit King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, the deadliest attack in Saudi Arabia in over a decade, and struck it again the next day. The dead came from Saudi Arabia, Bangladesh, the US, Egypt, Jordan, Syria, Palestine and Sudan.

  • Flights — over 600 flights were cancelled and operations were suspended; an Indian national was among the injured.
  • Warning — the Houthis told airlines to stay away from all Saudi airports except Mecca and Medina.
  • US response — the US president said America may join Saudi strikes, but also that it will not attack Iran before the November midterm vote.
  • Indian sailors — 2 Indian crew are missing after a drone hit the tanker MT Petro Dolphin; all 11 Indians on MT Valthera are safe.
  • Riyadh — the World Petroleum Congress opened in Riyadh anyway; the Saudi oil minister said fear "doesn't exist" in the Saudi dictionary.

One view is that the Houthis will next push for Aden, which would let them threaten ships before they even reach the Red Sea, and force Europe-Asia trade around Africa. The opposite view is that the real fight stays at the Bab el-Mandeb coast and Aden holds for now; both sides agree the threat to Gulf airports, power and water plants is rising.

Ships cost more than the oil premium

Supertanker hire from the Gulf to East Asia passed $1.4 million a day, up 40% in a week. Vitol's chief executive said the oil crisis has become a shipping crisis.

India's answer: drill at home and pass on costs

India imports about 89% of its crude, and home output keeps falling. The Samudra Manthan plan pays for offshore drilling through FY31, but new fields are unlikely to produce before the 2030s.

  • Decline — home output is down 27% since FY12, and old fields lose 6-7% a year.
  • Cost — 1 deep-water test well costs $125-150 million.

India's share market

The longest losing streak in 25 years ended, but only big shares look tired; small ones still hold up.

In short
  • The Nifty broke an 8-week losing run with a 289-point rise on 9 October.
  • Big household names like TCS and HDFC Bank have given almost nothing for 5 years.
  • Home investors keep buying through monthly SIPs while foreigners keep selling.

Cheap, but for a reason

The Nifty now trades at about 16 times next year's expected profit, its cheapest since May 2020. Banks led the bounce after the RBI's rate rise.

One view says this is the buying chance: Amundi, Europe's largest fund house, says the end of the Iran war will be the turning point for Indian shares. The opposite view says cheap can get cheaper: Emkay sets a Nifty target of 27,000 a year out, but warns of 21,000 if crude stays high.

  • Foreign futures — foreign funds hold 10% of index futures as bets on a rise and 90% as bets on a fall, though they covered some bets on Friday.
  • Levels — option traders see support at 22,450-22,500 and resistance above 22,750.
  • Trigger — bulls want Nifty Bank back above its 20-day average.
  • Reliance — Reliance Industries hit a fresh 52-week low on 12 October.

Festive buying started fast in small towns, but dear fuel is already cutting flying and squeezing shops.

In short

Another argument runs over the Zorawar light tank: critics ask why the Army demands top-level armour on a light tank and why L&T builds so few. Supporters say the armour rule dates from 2021 and is needed against Chinese guns in Tibet. Reading between the lines, if the Russia-Ukraine war cools, makers of artillery shells for Europe could lose their best orders.
